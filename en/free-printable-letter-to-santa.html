My Letter to Santa

Destination: the North Pole

A simple template to copy

Write “Dear Santa” at the top and create four areas: my name and age, something I loved this year, a present I would enjoy, and a drawing for Santa. Leave one final line to thank the elves and sign the letter.

For a child who cannot write yet, ask each question aloud and record their answers word for word. Their own expressions are what make the finished letter special.

How to decorate the page

Use pencils, stickers, stars or a little cotton wool for snow. Keep the layout uncluttered so the child’s words and drawing remain the focus. An envelope marked “North Pole” completes the activity.

Turn their letter into a personalised reply

Photograph the letter before sending it. You will keep the child’s words and can reuse one detail in Santa’s reply, such as a passion, an effort or a particular question. That continuity makes the magic feel believable.

A quick checklist

  • Use a single sheet of A4 paper
  • Leave a large drawing space
  • Keep the child’s exact words
  • Photograph the finished letter
  • Do not turn it into a shopping list

Turn the printable into a Christmas tradition

Let the child choose colours, draw a present or add a star before writing. For younger children, an adult can write down their exact words so their personality stays in the letter. Add the date as well: the page can become a lovely memory to revisit in a few years.

When it is finished, place it in an envelope and create a simple ritual: use the family letterbox, leave it beside the tree or take a photo before it travels to the North Pole. The goal is not a perfect wish list, but a warm moment together.
